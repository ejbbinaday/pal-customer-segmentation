Results brief · pre-defence

Anonymous trip-purpose segmentation, as it actually stands

Eleven segments over 22.9 million bookings. Three results worth leading with, one that did not work, and the four claims that are easy to make and wrong.

What to lead with

Safe to state −74% Unassigned bookings · 9.58% → 2.47%

The largest known gap in the deliverable is closed

For months we have reported an unclassified bucket as the biggest actionable gap. Three quarters of it turned out to be Filipinos buying international economy tickets in the Philippines — a population that behaves distinctly, not merely a residue. It is now its own segment.

Holds up because the new segment differs on round-trip rate, stay length, lead time and connections — not only on price. 64% round trip and 24% connecting, against 38% and 5% for domestic leisure.

Safe to state 44 / 55 Segment pairs clearly distinct · median AUC 0.861

The segments differ on evidence the rules never saw

Every pair tested against fields deliberately withheld from the rules, with per-pair exclusion wherever an anchor would have leaked a rule back in. Forty-four pairs clearly distinct, eleven weakly distinguishable, none indistinguishable.

Say which column. That is the adaptive measure. On the strict measure — only the two unconditionally clean fields that survived the July leak audit — the median is 0.637 and eleven pairs fall below 0.60. The strict view is thin by construction, not by failure.

Safe to state 4,411,666 Short-lead bookings now visible · was 2,945,686

Turning Last-Minute into a flag exposed half again as much volume

As a segment it only ever caught what fell through eight higher-priority rules. As a flag it applies to every short-lead booking — including 864,292 overseas-worker, 315,333 corporate and 196,364 balikbayan bookings that were short-lead all along and invisible as such.

The general lesson is that a priority cascade hides every overlapping signal beneath the winning branch. This recovered 50% more volume without moving a single threshold.

But the flag does not ride along with every segment. Three of the eleven rules read lead time too, so a short-lead rate per segment is partly tautology: MICE (rule: lead ≥ 45 days) and Ultra Wealthy Leisure (lead ≥ 30) are 0% short-lead by construction, not by behaviour, and Corporate's 35.6% is partly circular — one of its two branches only admits bookings with lead ≤ 7 days. On the channel branch, which carries no lead-time condition, Corporate is 23.3%. Still above the 19.26% book average, so the behaviour is real at a quarter of its apparent size — quote 23.3%. The three counts above are on the old labels, which is correct for "what was invisible before"; under the new ones the same 4,411,666 bookings split 858,318 · 415,411 · 193,897.

The one that did not work

Do not claim improvement 0.730 → 0.728 Overseas worker vs balikbayan · old labels vs new

The weakest boundary is exactly where it was

This boundary splits 6.8 million bookings and was the motivation for the whole exercise. Tested by holding method, anchors and population fixed and varying only the labels, the new taxonomy is neutral on it — marginally negative, within noise.

And 0.608 → 0.72 is not a real comparison. The earlier 0.608 is a cell from the strict pairwise matrix; 0.72 comes from an isolated clean-pair test. Two different measurements. Like for like, the strict figure has moved 0.608 → 0.548.

Why. The only change touching this pair moves 40,000 bookings — 1.4% of the branch. The stay-length discriminator that motivated the work is still a soft prior and still changes no label.

The number people will get wrong

Quote the second figure 23.4% Genuinely reclassified · 5,358,355 bookings

Not 62.7%

62.7% of labels differ textually from the previous run, but 39.3 points of that is only the Budget/Adventure → Leisure rename. The real reclassification is 23.4%.

Why it matters: 62.7% invites the question "so the model changed completely?" and the answer is no. Lead with 23.4% and the rename becomes a footnote rather than a challenge.

Validation, with the honest reading

State the limitation first

The segment label adds little on top of the raw features

Against held-out outcomes the label alone scores 0.60 on both stable measures — real signal, well above a coin flip. But added to the features it contributes +0.0005 on whether a booking flew and +0.0024 on rebooking within 180 days.

This is expected, not a failure. The label is a function of the features, so the features already contain it. It is precisely why the value of this work is targeting and communication rather than prediction — and saying so first is stronger than being asked.

Name the method 1.24 · 0.89 Transfer ratio against a within-window ceiling — GMM · LCA

Last year's model still carves this year's data — on the method that transfers best

Segment sizes and composition hold across a twelve-month step on full-population counts, and what drift there is sits in the three smallest segments, where a few hundred bookings move a mean. Treat that as unresolved noise, not established behavioural change.

Where it is weaker than it looks: the two methods land on opposite sides of 1.0 — GMM(full) transfers at 0.740 against a 0.595 ceiling, while LCA transfers below its own ceiling at 0.648 against 0.726. So say "on the best-transferring method", not "on this evidence". An annual refit buys little, not nothing.

Withdrawn: the earlier LCA figures — 0.729 against 0.645, ratio 1.13 — do not reproduce. That run's per-window sample was silently 43% of intended, and a ratio that crosses 1.0 when the sample doubles was never a finding. Outcome fields are excluded throughout as right-censored — recent bookings have not flown yet, and comparing them would manufacture a cliff.

Re-verified 18 August 2% Prevalence floor — blind below roughly 1%

We would find a planted segment at 2% of bookings, and we would miss one below 1%

Re-run against the new taxonomy at k=11 and the conclusion is unchanged. Reading the majority of the twelve method × archetype combinations rather than the luckiest one: detected at 2% prevalence from distinctness ≈0.494, at 5% from ≈0.219, at 10% from ≈0.13 — and never, at any distinctness tested, at 0.5% or 1%.

The bounded null, and the gap that travels with it: no segment exists in these features at or above 2% of bookings with distinctness ≈0.494 or greater. But a segment below ~1% — roughly 229,000 bookings, commercially meaningful — could exist unseen. Do not quote the single-method minimum of 0.114; groups as distinct as 0.567 were missed elsewhere in the same grid.

One instrument failed its own control. The H0 component count returned median 2 but a maximum of 131 across 100 draws of unchanged data, so no detection conclusion rests on it. That qualifies July's "1 significant H0 component" — the continuum reading survives as the centre of a noisy distribution, not as a measurement.

Domain finding from the SME programme

Present the pattern, not the cause 19.1% vs 8.5% Gulf round trips at 28–32 nights vs 12–16 nights

Manila–Gulf traffic runs on a one-month clock no other corridor has

It is the only corridor where a month-long trip outweighs a fortnight. Every other corridor sits at 0.60 or below on the same ratio. Revenue Management attributes it to employer-mandated leave for overseas workers.

The confound is open. If Gulf economy fares carry a one-month maximum-stay condition, the fare rule produces an identical pattern. The field that would settle it has been requested and has not arrived. Describe the rhythm; do not assert the reason.

Two parts of the SME's claim did not survive testing: there is no excess at 45 nights, and pooling Hong Kong and Taipei with the Gulf drops discrimination below chance — 0.375 against 0.676 for the Gulf alone.

Across the programme: 39 rules received, all 24 follow-up questions answered, 57 rules transcribed with provenance and live firing counts, and all six rules marked certain now asserted at build time. A first draft of the waterfall satisfied only four of the six — ordering alone does not implement a prohibition.

Scoring the 21 live tendencies against our own labels: they are silent on 43.6% of the book, and where they do make a call they agree with us 70.5% of the time. Their largest disagreement independently supports dropping Last-Minute as a segment.

Limitations worth owning before being asked

  • No loyalty field. Mabuhay Loyalist sits at 0.03% of bookings because we can only see award redemptions. The segment is real; our ability to see it is not.
  • No ancillary revenue anywhere in the extract, which understates premium and leisure segments specifically.
  • The customer base is a continuum, not a set of natural clusters. That is an established finding, re-tested six ways, and it is why rules label and machine learning validates rather than the reverse.
  • Blind below roughly 1% prevalence — any segment smaller than about 229,000 bookings could exist without us detecting it.
  • Two sampling defects were found and fixed on 18 August, after the validation numbers were first written. A filter placed above a reservoir sample returned n × selectivity rows rather than n, so V4 ran on 43% of its intended sample and the sub-segmentation ran on as little as 5% of its own. Both are corrected and re-run; V4's transfer figures changed as a result. Neither touched a segment label.
  • The build still moves by one booking between runs. 1,830 bookings have tied sort keys; cause and fix are recorded. Immaterial at one in 22.9 million, but it is a known defect rather than an unknown.
