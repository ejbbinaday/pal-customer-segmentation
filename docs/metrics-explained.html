How to read the strips

Every metric below sits on a strip. The left end is the worst possible result — usually "no better than a coin flip" or "nothing changed at all". The right end is perfect. The green line marks the value we'd want; the teal marker is where we landed.

worst case — the metric's floor ideal ↑

A marker close to the green line is good. A marker near the left end means the measurement couldn't tell anything apart. Where the ideal is unreachable in principle, the strip says so rather than implying we fell short.

Question 1 of 5 · construct validity

Are these actually different groups of travellers?

The segments were drawn by rules, so of course they differ on the rules. The real test is whether they also differ on evidence the rules never saw. They do — 44 of 55 segment pairs are clearly distinct, and none are indistinguishable.

Pair separability (AUC), full evidence set

Strong 0.861

What it measures: show the model one booking from each of two segments, without telling it which is which. How often does it pick correctly? 0.5 means it is guessing. Our median across all 55 pairs is 0.861 — right roughly 86 times in 100.

0.50 · coin flip1.00 · perfect ↑

Read as: the segments describe genuinely different booking behaviour, not just different rule outputs. Range across pairs 0.611–0.982; 11 pairs are weakly distinguishable but zero are indistinguishable.

The same test on only the two unimpeachable fields

Thin by design 0.637

What it measures: the identical test, restricted to the only two fields that could not possibly leak a rule — booking month and how often the customer books. Two fields cannot separate much.

0.50 · coin flip1.00 · perfect ↑

Read as: a floor, not a failure — and always say which of the two numbers you are quoting. The strict figure is low because the evidence available to it is thin, which is a consequence of being careful about leakage.

The weakest boundary: OFW/Migrant vs Balikbayan/VFR

Unresolved 0.548

What it measures: the same separability test on our single hardest split — 6.8 million bookings distinguished by essentially one bit of information.

0.50 · coin flip1.00 · perfect ↑

Do not claim this improved. An A/B holding method and population fixed and varying only the labels gave 0.730 (old) versus 0.728 (new) — the taxonomy change was neutral here. Overseas workers and homecoming visitors book too similarly for the data we hold to separate them.

Question 2 of 5 · criterion validity

Does knowing a customer's segment let us predict anything new?

Almost nothing — and that is expected rather than disappointing. The segment label is computed from the booking data, so a model that already has the booking data has already got the label's information. The deliverable's value is targeting and shared language, not forecasting.

What the segment label adds on top of the raw data

Near zero +0.0005

What it measures: predict whether a booking is actually flown. Using the raw booking fields alone scores 0.906. Adding the segment label on top moves it to 0.906 — a gain of five ten-thousandths. On repeat booking within 180 days the gain is +0.0024.

+0.00 · adds nothinga large gain ↑

Read as: do not sell this model as a prediction engine. It is a way of naming and targeting populations that the raw data already contains but nobody can act on directly.

What the segment label carries on its own

Real signal 0.598

What it measures: the same prediction using only the segment name — nothing else. 0.5 would mean the label is meaningless.

0.50 · meaningless label1.00 · perfect ↑

Read as: the label is not noise — it beats a coin flip on outcomes it was never built to predict. It just is not additional to the data it came from.

Question 3 of 5 · detection power

If a segment existed that we missed, would we have found it?

A hearing test. We planted fake segments of known size into the real data and checked whether the pipeline could hear them. Yes at 2% of bookings and above. No below about 1% — and that gap is a real blind spot, not a formality.

Smallest segment we can reliably detect

Verified 18 Aug 2%

What it measures: the smallest planted group that a majority of our twelve method combinations recovered. Smaller is better — it means a finer ear. At 2% prevalence we find it; at 1% and 0.5% we never do, however distinct the group is made.

↑ ideal · detect anything10% · very coarse

The limitation travels with the claim. A segment below ~1% of bookings — about 229,000 bookings, commercially meaningful — could exist and we would not have found it. Quote the majority floor, never the single best method run.

Question 4 of 5 · out-of-time stability

Does any of this still hold a year later?

Two adjacent twelve-month windows, compared on full-population counts. Sizes and composition hold. The model-transfer evidence is weaker than we previously reported — see the withdrawal below.

How much the segment mix shifted in a year

Stable 1.71 pp

What it measures: the share of bookings you would have to physically move between segments to turn last year's mix into this year's. 0 means nothing moved. Revenue mix is the less stable of the two at 3.36 pp.

↑ ideal · 0 pp, identical10 pp · substantial shift

Read as: the segmentation is not a one-year artefact. But note revenue moved twice as much as headcount — a segment holding its size is not evidence its value held. Report both or neither.

Does last year's model still carve this year's data?

Methods disagree 1.24 · 0.89

What it measures: fit on last year, apply to this year, and compare against the same method disagreeing with itself inside one period. That self-disagreement is the ceiling, not a baseline — so 1.0 means a year of time cost nothing. Our two methods land on opposite sides of it: GMM 1.24, LCA 0.89.

0.0 · a year destroys it1.0 ideal ↑  ·  1.5

Say "on the best-transferring method", not "on this evidence". An annual refit buys little — but one method says it buys nothing and the other says it would help. That is materially weaker than one clean number.

Can a model tell the two years apart at all?

Mild, as expected 0.621

What it measures: we ask a model to guess which year a booking came from. 0.5 is ideal here — it would mean the two years are interchangeable. Our controls make it readable: random halves of one year score 0.500, while domestic-vs-international scores 0.995.

↑ ideal · 0.50, interchangeable1.00 · totally different

Read as: the population has measurably shifted, but mildly, and the segment sizes absorbed it. Clear of the "no difference" rail, nowhere near the "obviously different" one.

Question 5 of 5 · production monitoring

Is the incoming data changing underneath the model?

The rules themselves cannot drift — they are code. Drift can only enter through the customers walking in the door. The segment mix is essentially frozen, and one input moved: PAL turned on a new booking channel.

Segment mix stability (PSI)

Stable 0.0028

What it measures: how much this year's distribution differs from last year's. 0 means identical. The industry convention: above 0.10 investigate, above 0.25 consider rebuilding. We are two orders of magnitude below the first threshold.

↑ ideal · 0, identical0.10 investigate · 0.25 rebuild →

Read as: no action needed. 22 of the 23 fields the rules read are equally stable.

Booking channel mix (PSI) — the one flag

Explained, not alarming 0.4111

What it measures: the same test on how customers reach us. It trips the rebuild threshold — but 93% of the movement is one new channel appearing: NDC went from 0 bookings to 366,890. Strip out channels that did not exist before and the same measure reads 0.0285 — stable. Alongside it, OTA fell 18.1% → 12.9% and direct web/app rose 32.9% → 37.2%.

↑ ideal · 0  ·  0.0285 excluding the new channel0.41 as measured →

A metric caveat worth knowing: this measure cannot tell "this category is brand new" from "this category exploded", so launching anything makes it cry wolf. The commercial question is real though: NDC now carries 3.6% of bookings and rising, no rule accounts for it, and booking channel is an input to the Corporate rule.

One number previously circulated is withdrawn

Earlier material quoted the year-on-year transfer test as 0.729 against a ceiling of 0.645 — ratio 1.13, read as "an annual refit buys nothing". That figure does not reproduce and should not be used.

It came from a run whose sample was silently 43% of what was intended: a database query applied its date filter after drawing the sample rather than before, so it returned about 13,000 rows per year instead of 30,000. Corrected and re-run, the same method gives 0.89 and a second method gives 1.24. That a result flipped sides when the sample doubled means the original was noise, not a finding. The defect is fixed, everything affected has been re-run, and no segment label was ever touched by it.
